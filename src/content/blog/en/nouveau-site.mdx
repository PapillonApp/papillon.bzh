papillon.bzh is Papillon's shop window. It's often the first thing people see of the app, before they even download it. And as of today, <Highlight>it has completely changed</Highlight>.

New design, new pages, a blog, an English version... So, what changed?

## The old site

The old site fit in three pages: home, association and download. A dark background with green highlights, a big title saying "Votre vie scolaire, avec de la magie en plus." (your school life, with a bit of magic), and an iPhone held in a hand to show off the app.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/ancien-accueil.webp", alt: "The old site's home page, on a dark background, with the title “Votre vie scolaire, avec de la magie en plus.”" }]} />

It did its job well. But over time, it wasn't enough anymore, in particular:

- **Too little room**: every feature fit into three blocks on the home page. No way to explain how the average is calculated, or what the schedule does when a class is cancelled.
- **No news**: nowhere to talk about updates or what happens behind the scenes, apart from our social networks.
- **French only**: even though Papillon is available in many languages.

How could we do better?

## A site that looks like the app

The first change is obvious right away: <Highlight>the site goes light</Highlight>. White background, black text, and Papillon green for the buttons. Just like in the app.

We also brought over the app's typeface, **SN Pro**, with the same weights as in our Figma mockups. Cards, corner radii and icons (our Papicons, of course) all come from the same design system. The result: going from the site to the app, you stay in the same world.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/nouveau-accueil.webp", alt: "The new site's home page, with Papillon on an iPhone and the title “La nouvelle vie scolaire”" }]} />

The home page still opens on a dark hero: a video of the app revealing itself, and a "La nouvelle vie scolaire" title with the app's emojis animating between the words. Then, within the first few pixels of scrolling, <Highlight>the page fades from black to white</Highlight>, and the navigation bar switches along with it.

## A redesigned navigation

The navigation was fully redesigned around four menus: **Features**, **About**, **Developers** and **Blog**. The features menu shows a preview of each page, with a piece of the app's interface.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/menu-fonctionnalites.webp", alt: "The open Features menu, with four colored cards: grades, schedule, homework and news" }]} />

On hover, a single highlight slides from one item to the next. And the download button hides a small menu, to pick your store directly or join the betas.

On mobile, the menu opens full screen, with a submenu for each section.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/mobile-accueil.webp", alt: "The new site's home page on mobile" }, { src: "/blog/nouveau-site/mobile-menu.webp", alt: "The mobile menu, open on the features" }]} />

## The home page

The home page was rewritten from scratch. It starts with a grid of animated cards that sum up Papillon in five words: **Connected**, **Private**, **Instant**, **Smart** and **Yours**. Each card has its own little live illustration: an account signing in, a masked password, homework being checked off, an average curve or a color picker.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/bento.webp", alt: "The grid of cards summing up the app, with their illustrations" }]} />

Then come:

- <Label icon="phone" color="green">Devices</Label>: iPhone, Android, iPad, Mac and iPhone Duo, each with its own screenshots.
- <Label icon="gears" color="blue">How it works</Label>: how to sign in, in four steps, without creating an account.
- <Label icon="lock" color="purple">Trust</Label>: open source, privacy and security, with our GitHub contributors.
- <Label icon="sparkles" color="orange">Reviews</Label>: a stream of App Store and Play Store reviews, along with our numbers.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/appareils.webp", alt: "The devices section, with one tab per device and Papillon on an iPhone" }]} />

The trust section is dark, right in the middle of a light page. And it's a small detail we're quite proud of: as you scroll past it, <Highlight>the navigation bar automatically turns dark</Highlight>, then goes back to light right after.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/section-sombre.webp", alt: "The dark trust section with three colored cards" }]} />

## A page for every feature

This is the biggest addition. Grades, schedule, homework and news now <Highlight>each have their own page</Highlight>.

And each page has its own color: green for grades, red for the schedule, orange for homework and blue for news. The whole page follows: the hero background, the icons, the buttons.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/notes.webp", alt: "The green Grades page" }]} />

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/emploi-du-temps.webp", alt: "The red Schedule page" }]} />

On each of these pages, we finally take the time to explain what the app really does: how the average is calculated with coefficients, how many points a grade adds or removes from your overall average, how a cancelled class shows up in your day... All with annotated screenshots, a section for large screens, and an FAQ for each feature.

## The association and our story

The association page had a rather general tone: some values, a team, but not much that was concrete.

You'll also find a map of France linking our five cities, an introduction to each team member and their school, and our commitments: free, open source, and never keeping your credentials.

## The blog

You're reading it! The site now has <Highlight>a blog</Highlight>, to talk about what's new in the app, what happens behind the scenes, and what we learn along the way.

Posts are sorted into three categories: **Articles**, **Development** and **Release notes**. Each one starts with a summary, and can be translated into English.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/blog.webp", alt: "The list of blog posts, with category filters" }]} />

## And also

A few other, more subtle changes:

- **Download**: a clearer page, with a QR code that takes you to the right store, every platform (iPhone and iPad, Android, Mac) and the betas.
- **In English**: the whole site is available in English, with a language picker in the navigation.
- **Animations**: all made with Motion, on a single shared curve, and toned down if your device asks for reduced motion.
- **Sounds**: small, subtle clicks accompany buttons and menus.
- **The 404 page**: a page that's skipping class, and a butterfly to catch.

<Photos outlined images={[{ src: "/blog/nouveau-site/404.webp", alt: "The 404 page, with a butterfly in place of the zero" }]} />

## Under the hood

On the technical side, the site is built with **Next.js 16**, **Tailwind CSS 4** and **Motion**. Every color and size goes through shared tokens. That's what lets dark sections and feature pages switch themes locally, without rewriting a single component.

Texts live in dictionaries, one for French and one for English, and blog posts are written in MDX. So adding a post or a translation doesn't require touching the code.

## Conclusion

The old site introduced Papillon. The new one takes the time to explain it: every feature, the team behind it, and everything that happens between two updates.

And above all, **it finally looks like the app it presents.**
